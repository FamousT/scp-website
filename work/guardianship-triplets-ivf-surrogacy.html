Legal Guardianship of Triplets Born Through IVF and Surrogacy

What it was

The children were born through IVF and a surrogacy arrangement, and DNA testing showed no genetic link between them and their father. Unable to rely on biology to establish their relationship with the children, the family pursued adoption. That application failed because essential evidence of the children’s conception, birth and almost six years of care had not been placed before the court. The children were then removed from the family by the Lagos State Ministry of Youth and Social Development and placed in a children’s home under a care order renewed every three months. The family faced a difficult question: how could parents with no genetic tie to children born through surrogacy secure their place in the children’s lives, when Nigerian law has no statute on surrogacy and recognises no parental order?

Why it mattered

Every month in care was time the children spent away from the only parents they had known. The case also addressed one of the least settled areas of Nigerian family law, the legal status of intended parents in surrogacy and IVF arrangements. It demonstrated that, with the right evidence and the right procedure, such parents can secure their children through the courts.

Our role

We took over the matter and rebuilt the case from the evidence up. We traced and procured the medical records of the IVF treatment and the surrogacy, which had been missing from the earlier proceedings. We placed before the court evidence that our clients had cared for the children, and bonded with them, since birth. We also called the medical director of the fertility clinic and the surrogate as witnesses, establishing the circumstances of the children’s birth beyond doubt. While the case was pending, we obtained an interim order granting our client visitation rights, which kept the family connected to the children; the reports of those visits were placed before the court. We secured a judgment of the High Court granting our clients legal guardianship and parental responsibility, and then moved an urgent application before the Family Court, which discharged the care order and ordered the children’s immediate release to their parents.

Secured legal guardianship and parental responsibility for a couple over triplets born to them through IVF and surrogacy, followed by the discharge of a State care order and the return of the children to their family.
