Our Work · Corporate Commercial & General Transactions

Writing the Rulebook for Nigerian Competition Law

What it was

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 gave Nigeria a competition statute but left the detail to subsidiary rules. Businesses and the Commission needed those rules to know how mergers are notified and reviewed, how dominance is assessed, and how a cartel member can come forward under leniency. We drafted them first as consultants to the World Bank and now directly for the FCCPC.

Why it mattered

These rules shape how transactions are reviewed, how cartels and other restrictive agreements are treated, when authorisations may be granted, and how dominance is assessed in Nigeria. With little local precedent to draw on, each instrument had to adapt international practice to the realities of the Nigerian market.

Our role

We wrote the rules: which deals get reviewed, when market power becomes abused, and how cartel members can come forward. Then we trained the officers who enforced them. Today, we advise clients under the same framework.

Outcome

More than 15 regulatory instruments drafted for the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC), which set how mergers are reviewed and how market conduct is assessed in Nigeria.
