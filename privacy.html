Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2026

1. About this policy

This policy explains how SimmonsCooper Partners (“SCP”, “we”, “us”) collects, uses, shares and protects personal data, and the rights you have over it. It is issued under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (the “Act”) and the regulations and directives made by the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (the “Commission”). It applies to personal data we process through this website, when you contact us, when you attend our events, and when we act for you or deal with you in the course of a matter.

We may update this policy from time to time. The current version is always on this page, with the date it was last updated.

2. Who is responsible for your data

SimmonsCooper Partners is the data controller of the personal data described in this policy. Our head office is at 9th Floor Fortune Towers, 27/29 Adeyemo Alakija Street, Victoria Island, Lagos. We have appointed a Data Protection Officer, whose details are in section 12.

3. The personal data we collect

  • a. Identity and contact data: name, title, employer, postal address, email address and telephone numbers.
  • b. Matter data: information about you that relates to a matter on which you instruct us or in which you are involved, including documents you give us.
  • c. Enquiry and correspondence data: what you tell us when you complete a form on this website, write to us, call us or meet us.
  • d. Recruitment data: your CV and other information you send when you apply to work with us.
  • e. Event data: details you give us when you register for or attend one of our events.
  • f. Technical data: your IP address, browser and device type, and the pages you visit. We collect usage data through analytics cookies only if you agree to them (see section 9).
  • g. Information from public sources, such as professional networks, directories and published material, where relevant to a matter or a business relationship.

We do not seek sensitive personal data (such as health information, religious belief or ethnic origin) through this website. Where a matter requires us to process sensitive personal data, we do so only on a basis the Act permits, such as your explicit consent or where it is necessary to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

4. Why we use your data, and our lawful basis

We process personal data only where the Act gives us a lawful basis to do so:

  • a. Contract: to take steps you ask for before engaging us, and to provide legal services under our engagement with you.
  • b. Legal obligation: to meet our obligations as a law firm, including client identification, anti-money laundering checks, record-keeping and professional conduct requirements.
  • c. Legitimate interests: to run and protect our practice, respond to enquiries, manage relationships with clients and contacts, and keep our systems secure, where those interests are not overridden by your rights and freedoms.
  • d. Consent: to send you newsletters and event invitations, to use analytics cookies, and to load Google Maps on our contact page. We ask for this consent with an unticked box on our sign-up forms and through the cookie banner. You may withdraw consent at any time.
  • e. Vital interests or public interest, in the limited cases where the Act permits it.

5. How we obtain consent

Where we rely on consent, we ask for it clearly and separately, and we record it. Consent must be a positive choice: we do not treat silence, inactivity, pre-ticked boxes or your simple use of this website as consent. You can refuse without losing access to the website, and you can withdraw consent at any time by using the “Cookie settings” link at the foot of every page, the unsubscribe link in our emails, or by writing to us. Withdrawal does not affect processing that took place before it.

6. Who we share your data with

We share personal data only where necessary and on a lawful basis, with:

  • a. our partners, lawyers and staff who need it to do their work;
  • b. service providers who support our practice, such as IT, hosting and email providers, under written terms that require them to protect the data and use it only on our instructions. The enquiry and newsletter forms on this website are delivered to us by email through Web3Forms (web3forms.com), a form-handling service based outside Nigeria, and careers applications are sent by the applicant from their own email account; the website itself is hosted on GitHub Pages;
  • c. counsel, experts, courts, regulators and other parties to a matter, where your instructions or the matter require it; and
  • d. public authorities, where the law requires us to disclose it.

We do not sell personal data.

7. Transfers outside Nigeria

Some of our service providers, including Web3Forms, GitHub and Google, store or process data outside Nigeria. Where personal data is transferred abroad, we do so only as the Act allows, for example where the recipient country or the recipient is subject to adequate data protection safeguards, or on another basis the Act and the Commission’s rules permit.

8. How long we keep your data

We keep personal data only for as long as we need it for the purpose for which it was collected, including to meet legal, regulatory, professional and accounting requirements. Matter files are kept for the period our professional obligations and limitation periods require. When we no longer need personal data, we delete it securely or anonymise it.

9. Cookies

We use strictly necessary storage to remember your cookie choice. We use analytics cookies only if you agree, through the banner shown on your first visit. Our contact page can show our offices on Google Maps; the maps load only if you agree, and Google may then set its own cookies. You can change your choice at any time through “Cookie settings” at the foot of every page. Some photographs on this website are served from Unsplash (images.unsplash.com), which receives your IP address when the image loads but sets no cookies. Our Cookie Policy gives the details.

10. How we protect your data

We apply technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, including access controls, encryption where appropriate, and confidentiality obligations on everyone who handles personal data. If a personal data breach is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will notify the Commission and, where the Act requires, you.

11. Your rights

Under the Act you have the right to:

  • a. be informed about how we process your personal data;
  • b. obtain confirmation of whether we process your personal data, and a copy of it;
  • c. have inaccurate or incomplete personal data corrected;
  • d. have your personal data erased, where the conditions in the Act are met;
  • e. have processing restricted in certain circumstances;
  • f. withdraw consent at any time, where we rely on consent;
  • g. object to processing, including processing for direct marketing;
  • h. not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that produces legal or similarly significant effects for you; and
  • i. receive your personal data in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format, and have it transmitted to another controller (data portability).

To exercise any of these rights, contact our Data Protection Officer (section 12). We may need to verify your identity before acting on a request. We respond without undue delay and within the time the Act and the Commission’s rules allow. These rights are subject to the exemptions in the Act, including legal professional privilege and our duty of confidentiality to clients.

If you are not satisfied with how we have handled your personal data, you may complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (ndpc.gov.ng). We would be grateful for the chance to resolve your concern first.
